## Design notes

* **One forecasting interface for two projects.** `Forecaster` is an abstract base class using the template-method pattern. `fit()` validates input once (length, NaN, minimum observations), and each subclass implements only `_fit`, `_predict` and `_fitted`. Backtesting (`walk_forward`), model selection and residual bootstrapping are therefore written once and work for every model, in P1 and P5 alike.
* **Inheritance where it models the domain.** `HybridMicroGrid` *is a* `MicroGrid` with a third source. It overrides `SOURCES`, `DEFAULT_A` and `solve_day` (adding a least-squares fallback for a singular system) and reuses everything else: cost model, dispatch, infeasibility handling.
* **Composition elsewhere.** `RiskAssessor` takes a `PriceModel` and a harvest plan rather than inheriting from either. Each `Region` is classified against a list of `CropRule` objects.
* **Dunder methods** where they read naturally: `__repr__` everywhere; `__len__` on series classes; `DistrictPopulation.__getitem__` for `kampala[2020]`.
* **Validation** raises `ValueError` with a specific message for empty, negative, mismatched or non-numeric input; the edge-case tests cover this.

## Summary of findings

**P1 — Population.** Wakiso grows fastest (CAGR ≈ 6.5%). On the 2022–24 hold-out, constant-percentage (CAGR) growth was most accurate for four districts and the linear trend for Arua, whose growth is slowing. Forecasts to 2029, with 95% residual-bootstrap intervals, imply about **4,800 additional classrooms** across the five districts (Wakiso ~2,090, Kampala ~1,550). The Fibonacci-ratio model implies ~62% annual growth and is off by about 80–90%; it should be retired.

**P2 — Micro-grid.** The system is well-posed (det = −5, cond ≈ 5.8). Demand errors can still grow up to ~6× in the dispatch, and a Monte Carlo check got within 0.3% of that bound. 4 of 30 synthetic days are infeasible: the critical load exceeds 4/3 of the daytime load, so the battery would have to be negative. NNLS repairs these with less than half the load mismatch of clipping. The battery is the more volatile source (CV 0.68 vs 0.25) and drives 62% of cost. Vectorised solving was ~30× faster than the loop.

**P3 — Fisheries.** The current h = 0.10 is sustainable but catches only 75% of MSY (rK/4 = 1,000 t/week). h = 0.20 attains MSY, while h = 0.30 depletes the stock to 2,500 t. A raw variance threshold is meaningless because variance is in UGX². CV is scale-free, and every scenario is *Moderate* risk, with 5% VaR ≈ 17% of expected annual revenue. A closed season helps only when the stock is over-harvested (+6% over 5 years at h = 0.30).

**P4 — Rainfall.** Cosine similarity is fixed (it is dot ÷ norms, not `math.cos`) and verified against SciPy. Cosine rates all pairs as "similar" (0.75–0.89) because rainfall is always positive; Pearson shows the seasons are not aligned. Peak detection with a dry-spell rule correctly classifies Kampala and Mbarara as bimodal and Gulu as unimodal. The advisory note recommends two bean crops a year for Mbarara.

**P5 — Taxis.** Ntinda's fare (UGX 2,000) is below its UGX 2,200 equilibrium, giving an excess demand of 10 passengers per trip-hour. In walk-forward tests, SES tuned to α = 1 (the naive forecast) beat the 3-day moving average and the trend on every route. Day-11 demand needs one vehicle per route. On 60 days with a weekly cycle, seasonal-naïve cuts MAE by about two-thirds compared with the moving average.

## Data sources
